01 — Research

What I want to spend a Ph.D. on

Large models are impressive in aggregate and unreliable in particular. My work sits at the point where that gap becomes dangerous: multi-step agents that fail silently, compressed models that quietly change who they serve well, and languages where no benchmark exists to notice either problem.

Uncertainty & Failure Repair

When a reasoning chain breaks, which step broke it? I study step-level uncertainty signals as a way to localize failures in LLM agents, and when targeted repair beats simply starting over.

Efficient & Fair Quantization

Quantization is treated as a lossless convenience. I investigate the mechanistic story — how low-bit compression rewires internal computation, and which of those changes induce bias.

Mechanistic Interpretability

Aggregate benchmarks hide mechanisms. I want to open the model up — attributing behaviour to circuits and representations rather than inferring it from output-level scores alone.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Retrieval routing, iterative feedback, evidence aggregation and agentic policies — evaluated head-to-head rather than assumed, in a domain where a wrong answer has a cost.

Low-Resource Languages

Bengali biomedical QA, Bengali sign language instruction generation. Building the first benchmarks for communities that current evaluation simply does not see.

Safety-Critical Deployment

First-aid guidance is needed exactly where connectivity is not. I build and rigorously evaluate assistants that must run on-device, offline, and never give dangerous advice.

Under review AAAI-27 LLM Agents

Uncertainty-Guided Error Repair in Multi-Step Language Model Agents: When to Target, When to Restart

Submitted to AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AAAI-27)

When a multi-step agent fails, should it redo the broken step or restart? A systematic answer across 3 benchmarks, 8 open-weight models and 33 repair strategies.

  • Scored every step of failed ReAct trajectories with five uncertainty metrics and six localization rules, including three novel cascade-aware rules that account for forward error propagation.
  • Found the optimal strategy is task-dependent: full restart beats even oracle-targeted repair on long multi-hop chains, while targeted repair with backtracking wins on shorter fact-verification trajectories.
  • Evaluated on HotpotQA, FEVER and MuSiQue over 7B–72B models with paired statistical tests, three seeds and a validated 72B judge.

Under review INLG On-device LLMs

HandsOnAid: Building and Evaluating an Offline First-Aid Assistant When No Help is Coming

Submitted to the 19th International Conference on Natural Language Generation (INLG)

A first-aid assistant that runs entirely on a mid-range phone — plus evidence that evaluation design substantially changes the conclusions.

  • Fine-tuned Gemma 2B with QLoRA on the 5,550-pair FirstAidQA corpus, deployable fully offline on a phone.
  • Rebuilt the test set around the deployment setting, replacing 68.3% of EMS-dependent references with expert-validated executable guidance.
  • Introduced blinded per-item scoring by three frontier LLM judges: fine-tuning gained 0.76 points on a 5-point scale, while retrieval added nothing beyond it and 4-bit matched 8-bit quantization.
Paper (PDF)
Accepted IEEE FMLDS 2026 RAG

Evaluating Retrieval-Augmented Generation Variants for Biomedical Multiple-Choice Question Answering

Accepted to IEEE International Conference on Future Machine Learning and Data Science 2026

Bangla had no large-scale biomedical QA benchmark — this work builds both the benchmarks and the retrieval systems evaluated on them.

  • Introduced BanglaMedQA and BanglaMMedBench — the first large-scale Bangla biomedical MCQ datasets.
  • Constructed a Bangla medical textbook retrieval corpus via OCR, combining textbook and web retrieval.
  • Benchmarked Traditional, Zero-Shot Fallback, Iterative Feedback, Aggregate and Agentic RAG; Agentic RAG reached 89.54% accuracy with gpt-oss-120b, with the strongest rationale quality.

Accepted NeurIPS 2025 Workshop First author

FirstAidQA: A Synthetic Dataset for First Aid and Emergency Response in Low-Connectivity Settings

Accepted to the 5th Muslims in ML Workshop, co-located with NeurIPS 2025

Saiyma Sittul Muna*, Rezwan Islam Salvi*, Mushfiqur Rahman Mushfique*, Ajwad Abrar  (*equal contribution)

The models that could help in an emergency are too heavy for the low-tier devices first responders carry, and no dataset existed to train lighter ones.

  • Released 5,500 expert-validated question–answer pairs spanning nine categories of first aid and emergency response.
  • Built an LLM-driven synthetic generation and validation pipeline — contextual chunking, filtering and human review for safety-critical instruction tuning.
  • Publicly released on Hugging Face to support offline-capable SLMs for emergency settings.

Accepted ICCV 2025 Workshop Vision-Language

Prompting with Sign Parameters for Low-Resource Sign Language Instruction Generation

Accepted to the ICCV 2025 Workshop on Computer Vision for Accessibility

Md. Tariquzzaman, Md Farhan Ishmam, Saiyma Sittul Muna, Md. Kamrul Hasan, Hasan Mahmud

Recognition solves only half the conversation; instruction generation teaches non-signers to produce gestures — and Bengali Sign Language had no dataset for it.

  • Introduced BdSLIG, the first Bengali Sign Language Instruction Generation benchmark, which also probes VLMs on genuine long-tail visual concepts absent from pre-training.
  • Proposed Sign Parameter-Infused (SPI) prompting, embedding linguistic sign parameters — hand shape, motion, orientation — directly into the prompt.
  • Produced markedly more structured and reproducible zero-shot instructions than free-form vanilla prompting.

Ongoing Interpretability Fairness

Building Fairness-Aware Quantized Large Language Models

Ongoing research project

Aggregate accuracy can hold steady under quantization while the model gets measurably worse for some groups — and nobody has explained the mechanism.

  • Investigating the mechanistic and explainability aspects of neural network quantization: how low-bit compression modifies the internal computations that drive fairness.
  • Analyzing benchmark quantization methods to isolate bias-inducing mechanisms rather than only bias outcomes.
  • Using those findings to design a fairness-aware quantization method that preserves efficiency without shifting who the model works for.
